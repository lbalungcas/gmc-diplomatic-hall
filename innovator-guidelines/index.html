Booth Submission Guidelines • Youth Innovations Marketplace

Use this checklist to submit assets for your Diplomatic Hall booth. Submit one package per booth and mark pending items as TBC.

Section 1

1. Overview of Booth Elements

Every showcase booth in the Diplomatic Hall combines physical presentation with virtual hall immersion. Review the core components and corresponding required assets below:

In the Hall What We Need From You Delivery Type
Booth Title & Category Official project name + category Text Form
Overview Panel Project summary (problem, solution, audience, outcomes) Text Form
Stats Chips Optional key facts (numbers + short labels, up to 4) Text Form
Links & Socials Website, pitch deck, and social profiles URLs / Links
Booth TV + Poster 30-second pitch video (or YouTube link) + poster image Media File / Link
Passport Quiz 2–3 short educational questions about your innovation Quiz Form
Hearts / Visitor Notes Built into the hall (visitors leave encouragement notes) Auto-Built
Section 2

2. Required Information (Text Content)

Please prepare the text assets below carefully. These texts populate your booth signage, interactive displays, and digital catalogue.

Project Identity

  • Booth Number: Assigned number or placement note (e.g., Booth #08).
  • Official Project Name: Maximum ~60 characters for crisp banner presentation.
  • Short Display Name: Optional short name for quick badges and cards.
  • Category: e.g., Innovation, GreenTech, EdTech, Health, Creative Arts, Civic Tech.
  • Theme: e.g., OCSEA, Child Marriage, Child Labour, Safe Schools, Environment.
  • Team Name: Full name of the youth-led group or initiative.
  • Country / Region: Primary country of implementation and geographical reach.
  • Supporting Organisations & Funders: Name and vector/high-res logo of partners.

Project Overview

  • Summary (80–150 words): Concise overview highlighting problem, solution, target audience, key outcome, and timeline.
Privacy Notice

Omit personal phone numbers, home addresses, or private details of minors and team members from public summary texts.

Optional Stats & Links

  • Stats (Up to 4): High-impact quantitative highlights (e.g., "12 Schools Reached", "3 Provinces", "Founded 2024", "5,000+ Youth Engaged").
  • Website & Pitch Deck: Public landing page URL or linked PDF deck.
  • Social Profiles: Full URLs to Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, YouTube, X, or TikTok.

Internal Organiser Contact

Ensure reliable contact channels for coordination during conference prep:

  • Lead & Alternate Contact: Full name, verified email address, phone number, and preferred follow-up language (English, etc.).
Section 3

3. Image Specifications

To ensure high visual fidelity in both the physical Marriott Diplomatic Hall screens and 3D Virtual Marketplace, submit images adhering to these exact dimensions:

A. Booth TV Screen Poster

  • Ratio: 16:9 widescreen landscape
  • Dimensions: 1920×1080 px recommended (min 1280×720 px)
  • Format: PNG or JPG, under 3 MB
  • File Name: booth_XX_screen.png
  • Design Tips: Clear title, high-contrast readable logos, keep critical text within center 80% safe zone.

B. Logo & Gallery Photos

  • Logo: Transparent background PNG, min 512×512 px (square or vector format)
  • Gallery Photos (Up to 4): Active shots showing innovators implementing or using the innovation in action
  • Photo Specs: 16:9 ratio, JPG/PNG, crisp resolution
  • Consent: Full written media consent for any recognizable children/participants
Section 4

4. Video Guidelines: 30-Second Pitch Video

Innovators introduce their innovation in a dynamic ~30-second pitch (maximum 90 seconds). In the 3D Virtual Hall and on booth TV displays, visitors click the booth TV for full audio while a silent video loop plays in the hall.

Recording Instructions

  • Setup: Landscape (16:9), well-lit and quiet environment with a clean, professional background.
  • Audio: Crisp vocals, minimal echo or ambient noise, no background music overpowering speech.
  • Format: MP4 file or unlisted YouTube/Vimeo link.
  • File Name & Size: booth_XX_tv.mp4 (<40 MB)

Pitch Structure (Suggested 30-Second Outline)

0 – 5s
Hook & Intro

Team name, project title, and the core problem being solved.

5 – 20s
Solution & Impact

How the innovation works and tangible positive change achieved for children.

20 – 30s
Call to Action

Invitation to visit the booth, test the solution, visit the website, or connect.

Section 5

5. Passport Quiz

Visitors unlock a gold passport stamp upon successfully passing your booth quiz (pass rate: 2/3 or 2/2 correct). This gamified experience drives booth traffic and ensures attendees learn about your solution.

Quiz Requirements

  • Quantity: 2 to 3 short educational questions related directly to your innovation or thematic focus.
  • Options: 2 to 4 multiple choice options per question.
  • Answer Key: Clearly mark the single correct answer for each question in your submission document.
Section 6

6. Safeguarding & Submission

Safeguarding & Ethical Compliance

Ensure active written consent for media featuring children, full rights to publish submitted assets, strict data privacy, and child-safe, audience-appropriate content.

Packaging Your Assets

Compile all media and documents into a single organized folder named according to this standard:

Booth_XX_ProjectName

Submit this folder via the designated cloud upload link provided by your focal point, or via email.

Youth Innovation Marketplace • Selection Guidelines

Official criteria, weighting breakdown, evaluation rubrics, and selection considerations for youth innovations preventing and ending violence against children.

Section 1

1. Purpose

The Youth Innovation Marketplace will showcase youth-led ideas, solutions, and innovations that contribute to preventing and ending violence against children. The selection process aims to identify innovations that are relevant, meaningful, practical, and ready to be shared with a diverse audience of partners, policymakers, practitioners, and other young people.

The Marketplace is intended to give young people space to present their solutions and perspectives, engage with stakeholders, and contribute to the broader Ministerial Conference dialogue.

Section 2

2. Minimum Eligibility Requirements

An innovation must meet all of the following minimum requirements to be considered for showcasing:

A. Youth-Led

  • The innovation is developed, initiated, or meaningfully led by young people.
  • Young people have a substantive role in designing, developing, implementing, or communicating the innovation.
  • Adult organisations, mentors, or partners may provide support, but should not be presented as the primary innovators.

B. Relevant to Marketplace

  • Violence against children and child protection.
  • Online safety and online forms of violence against children.
  • Prevention, response, recovery, or support for children affected by violence.
  • Child and youth participation; creating safer, inclusive environments.

C. Clearly Defined

  • The problem or challenge being addressed.
  • The proposed solution or innovation.
  • Who the solution is intended to benefit.
  • How the solution works and what outcome it seeks to achieve.

D. Safeguarding & Ethics

  • Appropriate for a diverse international audience.
  • Respects children's rights, dignity, privacy, and safety.
  • Has active consent for photos, videos, and stories.
  • Complies with applicable safeguarding and data privacy regulations.
Section 3

3. Selection Criteria (100% Total)

Eligible innovations will be assessed against seven core criteria with suggested weightings totaling 100%:

Criteria What Reviewers Should Look For Suggested Weight
1. Youth Leadership & Participation Meaningful youth ownership and leadership in the innovation 20%
2. Relevance to Child Protection Clear contribution to preventing or ending violence against children 20%
3. Innovation & Creativity New, creative, or contextually relevant approach to the identified problem 15%
4. Potential Impact Potential to create meaningful positive change for children, families, or communities 15%
5. Feasibility & Sustainability Practicality, demonstrated implementation, or realistic potential for continuation or scale 10%
6. Inclusiveness & Accessibility Considers diverse experiences, vulnerabilities, and barriers to participation or access 10%
7. Marketplace Readiness Ability to clearly communicate and showcase the innovation through pitch, visuals, or demo 10%
TOTAL WEIGHTED EVALUATION 100%
Section 4

4. Detailed Assessment Guide

Reviewers evaluate each application against the breakdown below to ensure thorough and fair appraisal:

4.1

Youth Leadership and Participation

20% Weight

Assess the degree and quality of youth ownership. Strong applications demonstrate that young people initiated or substantially shaped the innovation, are involved in decision-making, have meaningful roles in implementation, can confidently explain the innovation, and adult support complements rather than replaces youth leadership.

Key Reviewer Question

Is this genuinely youth-led, rather than an adult-led project being presented through young people?

4.2

Relevance to Child Protection

20% Weight

Assess how directly the innovation contributes to the objectives of the Marketplace and the broader goal of preventing and ending violence against children.

Strong Applications Demonstrate:
  • A clearly identified child protection issue;
  • A clear connection between the problem and the proposed solution;
  • Relevance to children and/or young people;
  • Potential contribution to prevention, protection, response, recovery, or systems strengthening.
4.3

Innovation and Creativity

15% Weight

Assess what makes the idea innovative, creative, or different. Innovation does not necessarily mean the idea has never existed before; it may involve a new approach to an existing problem, creative use of technology, novel ways of engaging children/communities, adaptation to a new context, or addressing gaps in existing services.

Key Reviewer Question

What is new, different, creative, or particularly relevant about this approach?

4.4

Potential Impact

15% Weight

Assess the potential contribution of the innovation to positive change. Consider who benefits, the reach (number or type of people), the significance of the problem, and evidence of results. Evidence does not have to be extensive: a small youth-led initiative can still demonstrate strong potential impact if the pathway to change is clear.

4.5

Feasibility and Sustainability

10% Weight

Assess whether the innovation can realistically be implemented or sustained. Consider whether it has already been tested, availability of resources or partnerships, practicality, capacity of the youth team, and potential for replication or scaling.

4.6

Inclusiveness and Accessibility

10% Weight

Assess whether the innovation considers the diverse experiences and needs of children and young people. Consider whether it meaningfully includes those who experience greater barriers/risks, is accessible to intended users, avoids discrimination, and considers gender, disability, socioeconomic status, and geography.

4.7

Marketplace Readiness

10% Weight

Innovations should be prepared to communicate their work to an international audience: explain the problem clearly, present solutions concisely, identify key messages, and deliver a short pitch. Note that teams receive dedicated prep sessions to refine their pitches before the Marketplace.

Section 5

5. Scoring Scale (1–5 Rubric)

Each criterion is scored on a standardized 1–5 scale, which is then multiplied by the criterion's percentage weight:

Score Description & Evidence Expectation
5 Excellent Strongly demonstrates the criterion with clear evidence, concrete examples, and outstanding youth ownership.
4 Very Good Demonstrates the criterion well, with minor gaps that can easily be addressed through preparation sessions.
3 Good Meets the criterion in fundamental aspects but requires further development and coaching to reach full potential.
2 Limited Partially meets the criterion; significant gaps remain in articulation, youth ownership, or feasibility.
1 Weak Does not sufficiently demonstrate the criterion or fails to address core requirements.
Section 6

6. Selection Considerations & Allocation

A total of 24 youth innovations will be selected for physical showcase display in the Youth Innovation Marketplace at the Marriott Manila Diplomatic Hall. Simultaneously, all eligible innovations submitted through the nomination will be featured in the Digital Marketplace, ensuring global visibility.

Mandatory In-Person Attendance for Physical Slots

To be eligible for physical Marketplace showcasing, each innovation must have at least two (2) youth innovators available to attend and actively present during the Marketplace event in Manila.

Diversity & Balance Allocation Criteria

The selection and allocation of the 24 physical slots considers:

  • Diversity of countries, regions, and contexts
  • Diversity of issues and child protection concerns addressed
  • Different types of innovations (both technology-based and non-technology solutions)
  • Different stages of development (from emerging concepts to tested interventions)
  • Representation of diverse groups of children and young people
  • Potential for cross-learning, collaboration, and scaling

Organising Team Nominations

To ensure balanced representation from each co-convening body, each organising team will have eight (8) nomination slots for the physical Marketplace (3 × 8 = 24 physical innovations total). All nominees must still fulfill eligibility and safeguarding criteria.

Section 7

7. Digital Marketplace

All eligible submissions will be included in the Digital Marketplace, regardless of whether they are selected for physical showcasing in Manila.

The Digital Marketplace provides a global platform for participating youth innovators to feature:

  • Innovation name, category, and thematic tag
  • Project overview and narrative summary
  • Key statistics and documented outcomes
  • High-resolution action photos and visuals
  • 30-second pitch video with interactive player
  • Direct links to project website and social channels
Section 8

8. Innovations That Should Not Be Selected

To preserve safeguarding, youth integrity, and conference alignment, an innovation will not be selected if it:

Disqualification Criteria:
  • Is primarily adult-led with limited meaningful youth involvement;
  • Has no clear connection to violence against children or Marketplace objectives;
  • Cannot clearly explain the problem it addresses or the solution proposed;
  • Presents significant safeguarding, privacy, consent, or ethical concerns;
  • Requires the disclosure of sensitive or identifying information about children;
  • Contains content that is inappropriate or unsafe for the Marketplace audience;
  • Cannot reasonably be presented or demonstrated within the Marketplace format.
Section 9

9. Expected Outputs from Selected Innovations

Each selected innovation should ultimately be prepared to provide:

Core Deliverables

  • Clear innovation title and thematic category
  • Concise description of the problem and solution
  • Key outcomes or evidence of impact, where available
  • Short youth-led live pitch and demonstration
  • Supporting visuals, posters, and presentation materials
  • Short pitch video (MP4 or link)
  • Relevant website and social media links
  • Formal confirmation of consent and safeguarding compliance
Youth Capacity Preparation Sessions

Selected teams will participate in tailored workshops to refine their pitches, finalize presentation assets, and prepare for engagements with international ministers, funders, and partners.
